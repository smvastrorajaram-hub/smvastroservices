Dasha and Bhukti in Vedic Astrology: Periods, Sequence and Balance

Learn the Vimshottari Dasha sequence, its traditional planetary periods, how birth-star balance is calculated and why timing should be read in context.

What Dasha and Bhukti mean

Dasha systems are traditional methods for organising planetary periods in a horoscope. Vimshottari Dasha is widely used in many Vedic astrology traditions and is based on a 120-year cycle assigned among nine planetary lords. A Dasha is the major period; a Bhukti (also called Antardasha) is a sub-period within it. Some reports display further subdivisions as Pratyantar or smaller periods.

These period names describe the structure of a traditional timing system. They do not guarantee that a specific event will occur during a period. A responsible interpretation considers the natal chart, the planet's role in that chart and the question being discussed.

The Vimshottari sequence

Planetary lordTraditional period
Ketu7 years
Venus (Shukra)20 years
Sun (Surya)6 years
Moon (Chandra)10 years
Mars (Mangala)7 years
Rahu18 years
Jupiter (Guru)16 years
Saturn (Shani)19 years
Mercury (Budha)17 years

The nine periods total 120 years. The sequence is cyclical. The starting Dasha at birth is determined by the Moon's Nakshatra under the Vimshottari scheme, not by the weekday or the calendar year alone.

Why the Dasha balance at birth matters

The Moon is positioned somewhere within a Nakshatra at the birth moment. The remaining portion of that Nakshatra is used to calculate the balance of its ruling planet's Dasha. A person is therefore not always shown as beginning life at the start of a full major period. The exact birth time and the Moon's calculated longitude affect the remaining balance.

When comparing two Dasha tables, check the birth data, ayanamsa, Moon's Nakshatra and the calculation of the balance. Small input differences may shift the period start dates, especially when the Moon is close to a Nakshatra boundary. Rounding the birth time or mixing settings can make a report appear inconsistent.

How to interpret a period carefully

Traditional readers usually begin by examining the Dasha lord's placement, house ownership, sign, strength, conjunctions and aspects in the natal chart. The Bhukti lord is considered in relation to the major period lord and the chart as a whole. Transits may be used as another layer, but they should not replace the birth-chart context.

A period lord can signify more than one topic because planets rule houses as well as carrying natural symbolism. This is why a short phrase such as “Saturn period means delay” is not a complete reading. The actual interpretive framework is more conditional and differs across schools.
