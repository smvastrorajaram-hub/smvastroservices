Lahiri Ayanamsa and Thirukanitham: Why Calculation Settings Matter

An introduction to sidereal zodiac calculations, ayanamsa, Thirukanitham and the input details needed for consistent horoscope results.

Educational guide · Published 11 November 2026

Tropical and sidereal zodiac references

Different astronomical and astrological systems can measure longitude from different reference points. A tropical zodiac is tied to the equinox reference, while a sidereal zodiac uses a stellar reference framework. The angular difference between these references is commonly called ayanamsa. A sidereal chart must therefore state which ayanamsa convention it uses.

Lahiri Ayanamsa is one widely used Indian sidereal convention. It supplies a defined offset for converting tropical longitudes to the sidereal framework. Other ayanamsa conventions exist, and their values are not identical. When a planet is close to a sign or Nakshatra boundary, even a small difference can change the displayed sign or star.

What Thirukanitham refers to

Thirukanitham, commonly associated with Drik Ganita in modern discussions, refers to an astronomical calculation approach that derives positions from computed celestial motion rather than treating every traditional table as a fixed input. The term is used in contrast with other traditional computational conventions, including Vakya-based methods. Exact implementations can differ, so a label alone does not tell a reader every detail of the software or ephemeris.

For a consistent report, identify both the calculation approach and the ayanamsa. “Thirukanitham” describes a calculation convention; “Lahiri” identifies an ayanamsa convention. They answer different questions and should not be treated as two names for the same setting.

Inputs that affect a chart

  • Birth date and local time: a small clock-time difference can alter the Ascendant and house cusps, especially near a boundary.
  • Historical time zone: local civil-time rules can change across years. A location's modern time offset should not be assumed for every historical birth.
  • Coordinates: latitude and longitude affect the local horizon and therefore the Ascendant and house framework.
  • Ephemeris and precision: astronomical data, rounding and interpolation choices can produce small differences.
  • House calculation: the sign positions and the bhava framework are separate parts of a report.

When comparing reports, keep the input data and calculation settings visible. A result that differs by a few minutes or a boundary sign should be investigated systematically rather than described as automatically wrong.

A repeatable checking method

  1. Use the same birth date, local time and birthplace in both calculators.
  2. Confirm that both tools use the same time-zone treatment and coordinates.
  3. Compare the stated ayanamsa, especially when checking the sidereal Sun, Moon or a planet near a boundary.
  4. Compare planetary longitudes before comparing chart labels or interpretations.
  5. Check house system and chart format separately from the planetary calculation.

This method makes differences easier to locate. It also prevents a common mistake: changing several settings at once and then not knowing which one caused the result to move.

What the current SMV calculation engine does

The published SMV calculation source sets the Lahiri sidereal mode before calculating planetary longitudes. The main calculation path uses Swiss Ephemeris data when the required ephemeris files are available and includes a Moshier fallback path. The browser-offline package uses a local WebAssembly calculation provider configured for Lahiri. These are implementation details of the current SMV software; they should not be inferred from the word “Thirukanitham” alone.

The workflow is: validate the local date/time and coordinates; resolve the local-time offset; convert the local clock reading to UTC and Julian Day; calculate sidereal planetary longitudes; then derive chart labels such as Rasi, Nakshatra, Pada and Navamsa from those longitudes. The SMV calculation-method guide includes worked examples and the current Panchang/Dasha formulas.

When comparing two calculators, compare the numeric longitude and stated ephemeris/ayanamsa settings before comparing a displayed sign or interpretation. A different ephemeris fallback or rounding policy can matter near a boundary even when both tools describe themselves as Lahiri-based.
